10 first games your kid can build this week

Every game here starts with one sentence. Your kid says what they want to build, gets a working first version, then changes one thing in the code and sees what happens. That last part is where the learning is.

1. Say itType the sentence into CodeIt, or change it to their own idea.
2. Play itPlay the first version. Something will be a bit off. Good.
3. Change one thingDo the change below and ask: what happened, and why?
1

Rocket dodge

Ages 6 and up

Say it
"A rocket that dodges falling asteroids."
They learn
Variables and lives: what lives = 3 means.
Change one thing
Set lives to 0. Why is it game over on the first hit?
lives = 3
2

Guess my number

Ages 7 and up

Say it
"The computer picks a number from 1 to 20 and tells me higher or lower."
They learn
Random numbers, input, and if / else.
Change one thing
Make it 1 to 100. How many guesses does it take now?
3

Rock, paper, scissors

Ages 6 and up

Say it
"Rock, paper, scissors against the computer, first to 3 wins."
They learn
Comparing choices and keeping score.
Change one thing
Make it first to 5. Which line did you change?
4

Star catcher

Ages 7 and up

Say it
"Catch falling stars with a basket, one point per star."
They learn
Adding to a score each time.
Change one thing
Make each star worth 5. What does + 5 do?
score = score + 1
5

Family quiz

Ages 8 and up

Say it
"A quiz about my family with 5 questions and a score at the end."
They learn
Text, questions and answers, counting right answers.
Change one thing
Add a sixth question. Does the score still work?
6

Dice race

Ages 7 and up

Say it
"Two players roll a dice, first to 20 wins."
They learn
Loops that keep going until something happens.
Change one thing
Race to 50 instead. How many more rolls does it take?
7

Virtual pet

Ages 8 and up

Say it
"A pet that gets hungry over time, and I can feed it or play with it."
They learn
Variables that change over time, and functions like feed().
Change one thing
Make it get hungry twice as fast. Which number did that?
8

Maze escape

Ages 9 and up

Say it
"Move a player through a maze with the arrow keys to reach the door."
They learn
Positions on a grid: x and y.
Change one thing
Move the door somewhere else. Can they still get there?
9

Typing race

Ages 9 and up

Say it
"Show a word, time how fast I type it, and show my best time."
They learn
Timing, comparing text, and remembering a best score.
Change one thing
Use longer words. Does the best time get worse?
10

Choose your path

Ages 8 and up

Say it
"A story where I pick left or right and it ends differently."
They learn
Branches with if, elif and else.
Change one thing
Add a third choice. Where does the new branch go?
